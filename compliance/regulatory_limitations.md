# Regulatory Limitations of Decentralized Trading Systems

---

## 1. Centralized Market Infrastructure

Indian capital markets are centralized and regulated.

All equity trades must route through:

- SEBI-registered brokers
- Recognized stock exchanges
- Approved clearing corporations

Decentralized smart contracts cannot legally settle equity trades.

---

## 2. No Direct Blockchain Settlement

Currently in India:

- Equity settlement is not blockchain-native
- Crypto tokens cannot represent NSE equities legally
- Ether or digital tokens cannot settle stock transactions

Thus, DABTP uses simulated Ether balances only.

---

## 3. Broker API Restrictions

Even algorithmic trading:

- Requires broker-level approval
- Must pass exchange certification
- Must implement kill-switch controls
- Must respect order throttling rules

A fully decentralized protocol bypassing broker controls
would violate exchange policies.

---

## 4. AI Risk Constraints

AI-driven trading systems must:

- Avoid market manipulation
- Prevent spoofing
- Prevent excessive order placement
- Implement exposure limits

DABTP simulates these risk rules on-chain.

---

## 5. Crypto Regulatory Uncertainty

India currently taxes crypto assets but does not classify them
as legal tender for securities settlement.

Hence Ether allocation in DABTP is purely virtual.

---

## 6. Practical Deployment Limitations

This system:

- Cannot connect to NSE live engine
- Cannot execute regulated equity trades
- Cannot bypass SEBI oversight

It can only operate in:

- Simulated trading environments
- Academic blockchain testnets
- Research-grade decentralized protocols

---

## 7. Summary

DABTP is a proof-of-concept framework demonstrating:

- Blockchain-enforced execution logic
- AI-suggested signal generation
- Transparent portfolio accounting

It is not a production-ready trading exchange replacement.
